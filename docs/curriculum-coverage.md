## What a learner can practice

Across the portals, the app provides on-demand lessons, vocabulary and grammar practice, reading, listening, writing and speaking trainers, mock exams, checkpoints, plans, and progress tracking. Availability and exam relevance vary by portal and level. In particular:

- The JLPT officially has five levels, N5 through N1. N1–N3 score language knowledge, reading, and listening; N4–N5 combine language knowledge with reading and score listening separately. Its CEFR reference varies by total score within N3, N2, and N1, and represents receptive skills only. The Japanese portal adds speaking and writing as broader language practice. See the [official JLPT level overview](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/levelsummary.html), [test sections](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/testsections.html), and [CEFR reference](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/cefr_reference.html).
- HSK 3.0 is now presented as a separate preview. The current HSK lesson library does not include a verified 3.0 course or new-framework scoring. CTI describes the new framework as three stages and nine levels, with five skills and translation at advanced levels on its [official HSK page](https://www.chinesetest.cn/HSK).
- Generated practice estimates are not official scores. Learners should use current exam-provider materials for registration, exact format, and scoring rules.

## Main missing lesson areas

1. **C1/C2 depth:** the advanced outlines now have at least five prompts per band and cover argumentation, dense listening, pragmatics, synthesis, and language variation. Review these sequences with teachers and add exam-specific examples, authentic texts, and assessed outcomes before treating them as a complete advanced course.
2. **Human-reviewed reference lessons:** generated lessons can vary and can contain factual or language errors. Create a small reviewed core per level before expanding the generated syllabus, then use learner reports to find recurring defects.
3. **HSK 3.0 course content:** develop a separate syllabus, skill sequence, sample items, and score interpretation from CTI source material before labeling any generated content as HSK 3.0 preparation.
4. **Coverage traceability:** connect every topic prompt and trainer to a named exam skill, level, and syllabus version. Review mappings when providers revise their formats.
5. **Learning outcomes:** state what a learner should be able to do after each lesson and include a short retrieval check plus a practical output task.

## Suggested content acceptance rule

For each level and exam skill, require a sequence of topic prompts with distinct outcomes, at least one authentic task type, reviewed target-language examples, and an explicit syllabus/version reference. Treat generated lessons as drafts until learners or a language reviewer report and confirm their quality.
